## The smallest useful story

A reviewer should be able to explain the actual change, its purpose or the limits of known purpose, one concrete before/after example, and an important consequence without assembling the narrative from scattered source. A short authored story leads with graph/source highlights and explains those highlights in plain English. It accounts for supporting files and exposes unknowns. It is not a generated call graph with decorative prose, a correctness verdict or an execution report.

The first implementation supports one selected story version per retained review snapshot, at most eight chapters and thirty-two steps. A useful initial story usually has three chapters: the reviewer-visible change, the important mechanism/consequence, and supporting changes/evidence/remaining questions. That is an authoring guideline, not a hard-coded generic story that fits every PR. Every chapter includes a brief why, what/how and concrete example. An unsupported why is explicitly unknown or attributed to its author; it is never inferred as fact from a function name or citation. A whole-PR account may admit that some files remain unexplained.

A step presents the graph highlight first and then the matching account, with both visible in one coherent application state. It may focus a verified static relation, a source/file/hunk comparison or a context-backed explanation. Authored rationale never becomes a compiler edge. Preserve before-left/after-right, red removal, green addition and yellow modification with non-color labels. An absent side stays absent; no before/after mode toggle or API/Helpers orientation selector is added. Repeated import-header excerpts are poor explanations: select actual relevant source/body spans and explain the decision they support.

## Explanation and investigation requirements learned from use

A useful static neighborhood can accurately show a new constructor dependency and unchanged neighbors while leaving the actual user consequence, request policy and lifecycle unexplained. Preserve that factual success and its limits. The story must supply an inspectable source-backed account of the decision and a concrete before/after user example, separating authored explanation from verified static bindings. An edge does not prove request authorization, teardown, business purpose or observed execution; missing support stays unknown. A source-corrected account is not evidence that the initial graph-first presentation already conveyed it.

Complete file inventory is necessary but cannot be the whole-change explanation. Use existing chapter membership and primary scope accounts to group the change by responsibility, with a concise source-backed reason for each group and visible unexplained material. Supporting docs/config/tests remain part of that account. No separate grouping taxonomy, inferred ownership engine or per-file semantic edge is needed. Acceptance requires a fresh reviewer to summarize central/supporting responsibilities and one real consequence without assembling a narrative from filenames and long function bodies.

Exploration initially shows issues associated with the exact selected declaration or source path; retain clear all-issues access and counts. Group only existing diagnostic codes/causes and distinguish incomplete-context findings from other reported issues without inventing severity or suppressing them. Entries with no reliable selected-source association remain explicitly unassigned/global, never falsely unrelated or resolved. This is a bounded projection over admitted facts and their existing coverage, not new analysis or a complete unresolved ledger claim beyond the returned data.

The source inspector foregrounds the exact selected span with side/path/commit/corpus/hash and applicable coordinate format, then offers full-file expansion. Preserve context needed to interpret the span and an explicit return to the original focus/scroll/selection. Highlighted-span selection and source availability are separate from question anchor permission; unchanged semantic-only context does not become a fabricated legacy snapshot anchor. The goal is less filtering and source scanning while keeping every available fact, warning and source route inspectable.

## Authority and provenance

All narration is authored content. The app validates identity, references, bounds and authorization; it does not prove that the prose accurately interprets its citations. The rail and step show who wrote the account, when, which immutable snapshot/version it concerns, and the basis of each claim. Keep these categories separate:

| Basis | Meaning and display |
|---|---|
| Source-linked observation | Author's statement linked to exact retained source. Valid coordinates/hash establish the cited source, not truth of the statement. |
| Static relationship | Exact admitted analysisId/edge or node plus source evidence. Render the system's static-method/certainty/coverage labels independently of the author's wording. No runtime execution or policy causality is inferred. |
| Supplied rationale | An attributed statement from an owner-supplied Epic/Ticket/PR capture or the identified human/agent author. Display origin and capture time; do not present it as independently verified intent. |
| Inference | Author's interpretation with source/context references and explicit uncertainty. It remains interpretation even when cited. |
| Unknown | Missing explanation/evidence with a concise question or what would resolve it. No invented fallback claim. |

Examples distinguish an illustrative input/before/after scenario from an observed result. In this slice all examples are illustrative or source-derived authored accounts. Test paths and test source can establish presence only; neither a story author nor a context paste can mint an execution receipt or a green “passed” badge. Actual execution results enter through the future evidence contract, not arbitrary text.

The context rail separates **Supplied context** (Epic, Ticket, PR material) from **Authored understanding**. Missing Epic/Ticket/PR body is shown as not supplied. Existing imported candidate title/URL/base/head may be shown as retained provider metadata, with its acquisition provenance; an imported title does not imply that the body or linked ticket was fetched. Owner-pasted context is a retained capture, not live-synced content. Captured content is untrusted text: never execute its instructions, fetch its URL, grant authority from it or treat it as agent/system policy. Use plain text with safe links; no raw HTML, embedded scripts, executable diagrams or remote image fetches.

## Immutable story and context values

Story identity is scoped by reviewId and retained snapshotId. ContextRevision and storyRevision start at zero and advance independently; graphRevision, analysisGeneration, jobRevision and reviewRevision retain their existing meanings. A story pins optional admitted analysisId/corpusId, exact context capture IDs and the contextRevision used for authoring. A story without semantic analysis can explain source/metadata but must show “static neighborhood unavailable” and cannot contain static-relation references. It still uses the existing structural graph/whole-change ledger.

Owner context writes create immutable captures with server-issued ID, kind Epic/Ticket/PR, title, plain text, optional safe HTTP(S) origin URL, server-derived actor/time and SHA-256 content hash. Capture metadata includes freshness `captured` or `unknown`; no elapsed time makes it authoritative/current. Updating a capture creates a new ID and supersedes reference. A context revision selects the retained capture IDs; old revisions remain readable for old stories. Agents can read captures but cannot impersonate the external source or change the supplied-context rail. A newer context revision labels older story context as superseded; it never rewrites that story.

A story version contains title, optional original questionId, snapshot identity, optional analysis/corpus basis, context revision/references, ordered chapters/steps, whole-change scope accounts and limitations. The server adds versionId, monotonically increasing storyRevision, content hash, author actor/kind/attachment generation when applicable and createdAt. The hash uses canonical JSON of the validated authored payload, recursively ordered object keys and preserved array order; it is not a new snapshot or analysis digest. Versions are immutable; publishing a correction creates a new version with previous-version provenance. Old questions, source, graph clarifications and stories never reanchor automatically.

Chapters have stable author-local IDs, title, why/what/how/example claim IDs and ordered step IDs. Each account resolves to the same provenance-tagged claim collection; no bare chapter rationale bypasses claim attribution. The example includes an illustrative/source-derived label and input/before/after claim references. Steps have stable IDs, headline, bounded claim IDs and a focus descriptor. Claims contain plain text, one basis from the table, references and optional attribution/uncertainty. A focus names exact source anchor(s), structural file/hunk IDs or admitted semantic node/edge IDs; it contains no arbitrary node coordinates/edges/source bytes. One step has at most two primary source anchors, one per side, with a separately identified comparison. Text-only unknown steps explicitly have no source focus. A graph layer may highlight a maximum of twelve visible nodes using existing readable-neighborhood policy; other facts stay accessible.

Reference types are strict tagged values: retained snapshot source (snapshot/side/path/contentHash and exact line span); semantic source (snapshot/corpus plus the complete semantic UTF-16/line anchor); static node/edge (analysisId plus existing fact ID); supplied capture (captureId/contentHash and optional exact text range); retained PR metadata field; or clarification ID plus its exact revision and analysisId. No mutable current pointer, local file path, prose URL or guessed identifier substitutes for evidence. Snapshot LF-based line spans and semantic parser coordinates remain distinct formats; use their owning validators, not arithmetic conversion that breaks CRLF/U2028/astral text. Validate every static and semantic reference through retained authorized04 read ports. Clarification text remains attributed interpretation when cited.

Each primary changed-file ledger key from `graphFiles(snapshot)` has exactly one scope account: a primary chapter with a short explanation, or an explicit unexplained reason. A file may be referenced by other steps without increasing coverage. Preserve copy/rename comparison keys and repeated identical provider source occurrences; never deduplicate the stored manifest or confuse a copied comparison with its modified original. Existing hunk/disposition coverage is rendered from source, not recreated or claimed narrated by story counts. Missing/binary/empty/unsupported files remain accessible scope rows; no fabricated line1. More than512 primary files is explicitly unsupported by this bounded story contract, never silently truncated into a whole-PR claim.

## Planned authoring and read protocol

The following new operations are design, not existing exports. Use strict public domain schemas, the existing SQLite owner, authenticated gateway ingress and the existing attached participant's MCP transport. No app-hosted model, API key, real-time service, new conversation or session fork is introduced. Context/story publication is a bounded durable value write, not a repository process job and does not acquire the shared execution lease.

| Operation | Input and apply semantics |
|---|---|
| PutStoryContext | User only: commandId, reviewId, snapshotId, expectedContextRevision and bounded capture create/supersede/selection intent. Validate retained snapshot and origin/text limits; append captures and context revision atomically. Agents/bootstrap cannot write supplied context. |
| PublishStory | User or active attached agent: commandId, reviewId, snapshotId, expectedStoryRevision, contextRevision, bounded complete story payload and optional replacesVersionId. Pin only retained source and admitted analysis. CAS storyRevision and the authoring context revision; reject stale context with `story_context_conflict`, stale story with `story_conflict`. Do not use unrelated graph/review/job revisions as CAS. |
| Story query | Authorized reviewId/snapshotId, optional exact versionId, minimumStoryRevision and minimumContextRevision. Return selected published version summary, requested exact version, current snapshot/analysis markers and explicit newer-version/context indicators; unmet minima are `revision_unavailable`. Query never moves a selected source/history pointer. |
| Story context query | Authorized reviewId/snapshotId and optional exact contextRevision/capture ID. Return bounded retained captures with provenance. No external fetch or arbitrary path read. |

Command IDs and author-local chapter/step/claim IDs are nonempty strings of at most128 characters; revisions are nonnegative integers. PutStoryContext has exactly one action: `create` with one capture (kind/title/text/optional originUrl/optional supersedesCaptureId), or `select` with an ordered list of retained capture IDs. Create atomically selects the new capture, replacing only its named superseded capture when present and preserving other selections; more than16 selected captures rejects. Select may remove a capture from the current rail but never deletes retained data. Duplicate/foreign/supersedes IDs reject. PublishStory supplies the complete authored value, not an arbitrary patch; every chapter/step/claim/focus/scope reference must resolve in that value or retained evidence. Example scenarioKind is `illustrative` or `source_derived`; neither author-controlled value permits observed-execution badges.

Each command has a bounded ID, canonical actor/payload fingerprint and durable receipt. Recheck authorization before receipt replay. Exact authorized retry returns the original result; changed actor/payload conflicts. Receipts contain commandId/actorId/applied-or-rejected, reviewId/snapshotId, storyRevision/contextRevision, optional new version/capture IDs, result and typed error. They are not graph/job receipts with a renamed revision. Append body, pointers and receipt in one short transaction after bounded validation/encoding; recheck retained membership/authorization/CAS inside. No transaction waits for an agent, browser, source process or external context.

Attached agents may publish only for their authorized review and current attachment generation, as derived from credentials. They may create an initial story or revise the story series they authored; its initial author remains the series owner when a user appends a correction. A user may supersede any version with preserved provenance. A different attached author receives `story_author_conflict`; the user can publish an attributed account through owner ingress instead. Transfer of series ownership and collaborative authorship are deferred, not silently inferred from attachment replacement. Bootstrap is never an author. Do not allow caller-supplied actor/timestamp or `verified:true`. Replacing attachment does not alter story authorship, and revoked generation cannot read/write/replay through the former capability.

A new graph clarification or analysis request does not conflict with a story write. A story may cite a retained earlier admitted analysis explicitly; publication labels that basis historical if it is no longer selected. Source history advancing likewise does not invalidate a historical story. Publication updates the snapshot's available current story pointer but never auto-selects it in an already-reading client. The client announces a newer story/context and offers an explicit open action. If an older reference is unavailable/corrupt, reject new publication; existing readback reports broken evidence rather than substituting current source or invented text.

### Addressed explanation requests and meaningful answers

Reuse the existing AskQuestion → durable addressed inbox/CLI delivery → same-session MCP read/reply flow. “Ask for an explanation” collects the user's question and an actual selectable snapshot source anchor, then submits one normal AskQuestion intent through the review bridge with the new bounded recovery contract below. The reviewed028 bridge stores pending identity only in memory; recoverable reload is required05 work, not an existing04 capability. Its visible request text includes the immutable review/snapshot, chosen analysis/context/story revisions and the bounded requested artifact; the original question remains explicit. It does not create a new correction inbox, notification type or background authoring job. When no selectable source exists, retain normal source limitations and permit owner publication; do not invent an anchor or fake delivery.

The attached agent reads the actual question and source plus new story/context read tools, writes through `story_publish`, and separately answers the original question with existing `reply_submit` evidence/limitations. Publication may link the exact questionId from the same review/snapshot, but does not mark it answered or establish that the answer is useful. A generic SDK reply that merely acknowledges transport cannot pass the explanation task. The author must address the user's trigger/why, before/after, consequence and limits with relevant evidence; independent review judges that quality. The content protocol does not confer native automatic tool-catalog/delivery capability. Controlled owner/MCP fixtures prove only their exercised engineering path; missing native Codex/Claude or unfamiliar-human evidence remains open.

Expose new MCP `story_get`, `story_context_get` and `story_publish` through the already attached capability, deriving review ownership as in current tools. No host configuration/install or participant replacement belongs to story authoring. Ordinary story/context publication, player movement, source exploration and replies do not wake the agent again. Only the explicit user question uses the existing addressed-request delivery route; unknown delivery retains its existing non-replay policy.

## Resource bounds and persistence

Bound text and structures before expensive reference resolution: at most16 current captures,8 KiB text per capture,128 KiB encoded context command; eight chapters,32 total steps,128 total claims, eight references per claim/step and256 unique evidence references per story; title/headline120 characters, claim1200, scope explanation400, total encoded story command256 KiB. All IDs/references are finite strict values; malformed arrays/duplicate local IDs/dangling steps/chapter or scope refs reject. Context/story inputs cannot raise server limits. The existing8 MiB gateway envelope is not permission to accept unbounded authored output.

Retain at most8 MiB canonical encoded context capture/revision and story-version bodies per review, counting each immutable body once, including text/escaping/provenance/scope/facts references. Exact boundary passes; the next new write rejects atomically with `story_storage_limit`, retaining prior history. Owner test limits may only lower. No silent eviction, pruning of cited evidence or automatic compaction of a story into different content. Receipts, private browser state and the rest of SQLite are separate from this body quota; this is not an all-database resource guarantee.

Use new owner-held story/context tables and receipts through `createStore`; other subsystems never open SQLite or read storage internals. Source/semantic evidence validation uses bounded public ports and indexed lookups once per unique reference. Retained reference facts are immutable and may be checked outside the transaction; authority/CAS/body equality recheck inside. Domain owns content policy and transitions, application owns view/player state, UI renders. Existing import/analysis process leases, schemas and resource limits stay unchanged. Restart reads authoritative versions/receipts without rerunning acquisition, analysis or agent turns.

## Player, reading and exploration

The slim bottom-center player has one row: drag handle, previous, replay/pause, next, step number and chapter selector. Icons have accessible names/tooltips; text carries story information. Keep44px coarse-pointer targets even when visual chrome is slim. Controls remain operable at narrow width; chapter labels may shorten with full accessible names, never hide step/navigation. A keyboard move/reset alternative makes dragging optional. Position is private, clamped inside the visible viewport/safe-area, restored on resize and resettable; dragging never navigates or alters shared state.

Player state is application-owned: exact review/snapshot/storyVersion, chapterId/stepId/index, mode (`paused`, `playing`, `exploring`, `ended`), elapsed step time, highlight/displayed evidence, selected comparison/source and an optional exploration return frame. Initial load is paused. Explicit Replay replays the current step's highlight from time zero and continues in story order across chapter boundaries; while playing the same control is Pause. Previous/next moves one step in global story order, chapter choice selects that chapter's first step, and either pauses for reading. Step number shows the global position/total with the current chapter label. Default playback advances after eight seconds of visible active time per step; the final step ends paused/ended, with no loop. No duration controls, audio or authored executable timing language are added.

Reading actions pause: focusing/selecting narration (not focus inside the playback controls), user scrolling the source/context rail, activating evidence, dragging the player, switching Flow/Diff, hiding the page or losing the active review connection. Preserve elapsed time; no hidden-tab catch-up. Reduced-motion uses immediate highlights and starts paused, and never removes information. Replay is an explicit time-zero action; “Resume story” after exploration restores its saved cursor/elapsed value in paused mode, so it cannot advance before the reviewer is ready.

Entering exploration first freezes one return frame containing the exact story version/chapter/step/elapsed cursor, graph focus/expanded IDs/viewport, displayed source/comparison/selection and original draft identity/anchor/text. Exploring other nodes, source, chapters through ordinary source navigation or supporting files does not overwrite that frame; explicit story navigation exits exploration after restoring/confirming pending draft rules. Resume restores the frame, not the latest current graph or newest story. Exploration can have its own private selection/draft; never replace a dirty or uncertain submitted draft with the saved one. Preserve both scoped drafts and require explicit choice/reconciliation when switching which composer is active. The pending-submission recovery contract below requires exact unchanged retry/readback before retargeting, including after reload.

Story focus and question draft anchor are distinct. Playback may change displayed evidence through an explicit user play/step action, but cannot silently retarget an existing question/draft. “Ask about this step” is a separate intent and respects pending/dirty composer state. Import completion, analysis admission, context/story publication and polling never move the story cursor, active source snapshot, private selection or draft. Explicit user history/version change pauses and stores the old private frame; opening a newer story starts paused rather than borrowing old chapter IDs.

Basic mobile Flow (graph/story) and Diff (source) presentations share the same application state. Switching is reading/exploration, not a second story instance; preserve cursor, pause, return frame and draft. Before/after identities stay available on narrow screens without a toggle that changes source identity. Full mobile tuning, semantic zoom, sophisticated motion and4K polish remain later scope.

Retain a bounded tab-session view checkpoint (sessionStorage, at most64 KiB encoded UTF-8) of private IDs/cursor/position/return-frame and unsent draft text (at most8 KiB per active/return draft), never bearer credentials, full source, story/capture bodies or process data. This is tab-local recovery, not account sync or shared canonical state. On reload validate against authorized retained records and pause before showing; unavailable/stale selection gets explicit recovery without guessing an anchor. Oversize unsent drafts remain complete in memory, are omitted from this view checkpoint and visibly marked unavailable after reload; never truncate a draft. If storage is unavailable/full, unsent view state can remain in memory with an explicit recovery-unavailable message. This best-effort rule does not permit sending an uncheckpointed question.

### Pending question recovery across reload

The review application owns a new public recovery seam, separate from story publication and the import bridge. The minimum contract is `getQuestionRecovery()` (safe state/identity), `recoverPendingQuestion()` (explicit action under the current authenticated credential) and the existing `ask` operation extended with write-ahead persistence. These names are proposed05 public exports, not claims about028. UI passes intent through them; it never reads/writes pending storage directly or reconstructs an AskQuestion command from a restored draft. No new server job, inbox category, credential service or receipt endpoint is required: retry uses the existing `/api/commands` receipt path; readback uses the authorized retained-review query.

At most one unresolved question command is retained per tab. Before any first network send, the bridge validates the immutable anchor and text using the existing AskQuestion policy (trimmed nonempty text at most16 KiB UTF-8), constructs the complete command once, and persists its exact serialized request and SHA-256 fingerprint. The request includes type, commandId, reviewId, original snapshot/anchor, exact text and optional parentQuestionId. Preserve original bytes on every retry; the server retains its own canonical normalized receipt fingerprint. These are distinct hashes, not interchangeable proof of acceptance. The checkpoint also contains schema version, private originalDraftId, credential scope, phase, and any accepted receipt. The public ask intent carries this stable private draft identity separately from its server command; it is not an added AskQuestion wire field. It contains no bearer, full source or copied story. Cap the request at128 KiB and the entire encoded pending record at256 KiB, independently of the64 KiB view checkpoint. Bounds include JSON escaping; oversized metadata or raw whitespace rejects before send rather than truncating or weakening existing server validation.

Persist one complete record with atomic sessionStorage replacement, then read it back and verify equality before dispatch. Unavailable storage, quota failure or failed equality means **unsent**: retain the draft and show recovery storage unavailable; no network mutation occurs. Unknown-schema/corrupt pending records are quarantined visibly and cannot be converted into an ordinary resubmittable draft. A hash detects accidental mismatch, not malicious storage tampering; the server remains authoritative for all commands and authorization. Browser data deletion, tab closure and independent duplicate tabs are outside this tab-reload recovery guarantee; do not claim global duplicate-intent detection.

Credential scope is the SHA-256 of a domain-separated, length-delimited tuple containing the gateway origin and the exact trimmed bearer used for authentication, computed in memory. Persist only this hash. It is a local matching guard, never an authentication credential or an authority assertion; actorId alone is insufficient because multiple credentials may name the same actor. The authenticated public review read must succeed before recoverable draft details are hydrated or a recovery action is enabled. The token always remains memory-only. A new login increments the live bridge generation and invalidates old promises; matching the stable scope allows explicit recovery across that generation change, never automatic adoption/send. Capture the in-memory credential and live generation for each effect; recheck generation and pending fingerprint after checkpoint work and immediately before dispatch. Never read a later mutable token to send an earlier intent. Response/checkpoint writes likewise require the captured generation and exact pending identity. A different credential, origin, unauthorized review or failed authentication leaves the pending command locked and sends nothing, even if the actor name matches. Offer re-entry of the original credential; never migrate/rekey or mint a new command on the user's behalf.

| Pending phase | Required behavior and transition |
|---|---|
| `awaiting_ack` | Persisted before first send, so crash-before-send and lost acknowledgement are conservatively indistinguishable. Reload shows unresolved submission, with no auto-resend. After explicit authorized same-scope recovery, send the exact stored bytes and commandId through normal ingress. The same server receipt is returned if already committed. Editing a composer cannot change this record or create a replacement command. |
| `accepted_awaiting_readback` | On an applied receipt, retain the exact receipt including commandId/actorId/revision/cursor/questionId/eventId and original payload, and checkpoint this phase before reporting completion. Recover by reading the original retained snapshot with minimumReviewRevision equal to receipt.revision. Match questionId, actor, normalized text, full anchor and optional parentQuestionId; revision alone is insufficient. If a persisted receipt cannot be validated, retain unresolved state rather than fabricate completion. |
| Confirmed applied | Only matching authorized readback completes the submission and removes the pending record. Mark the original draft as submitted; it cannot reappear as an ordinary unsent draft from an older view checkpoint. Update/invalidate that draft checkpoint before removing the pending record; if either storage update fails, retain the pending record so another explicit recovery is harmless. Do not erase an unrelated exploration draft. |
| Confirmed rejected | A matching authoritative rejected receipt for this exact command can release the pending guard and retain the draft for an explicit corrected new intent. A `command_conflict`, identity mismatch, malformed reply or transport/authentication error does not prove the original unknown command was rejected; keep it unresolved. |

If persisting the applied-receipt phase fails, keep the already durable `awaiting_ack` record and the receipt in memory. Explicit retry after reload still uses the same command; it cannot append a second question/event. Never clear a previously uncertain command merely because a later attempt returns HTTP400/401/403. If accepted readback fails or is stale, preserve the accepted revision/receipt and retry reads without resending; no new ID. No caller may turn an unresolved pending record into a fresh submission by changing text, source, story, login or component lifetime.

Recovery reads are keyed to the original command's review/snapshot and captured credential generation. They reconcile status independently of the currently displayed historical source; they do not retarget the composer, move the story cursor or replace the selected snapshot. Preserve the existing pending guard against new question submission/source retargeting. Reloading into another history URL may display that history read-only while the pending command remains visibly recoverable for its original source. A late response from an old login/disposed bridge cannot update the view, clear a newer record or attach an old receipt to another command. Reading the original source for reconciliation is not implicit navigation to it.

Explicit logout clears the bearer, invalidates the generation and removes the ordinary view checkpoint. Retain the noncredential pending record in a locked state until explicit same-scope recovery; a “logged out” view reveals no saved draft body. This narrow exception prevents logout/login from making an uncertain question look unsent. Existing import explicit-login intent-clearing remains unchanged: it does not own or clear this review-question recovery record. Closing/clearing tab data can discard local recovery, so the app must never label that discard as a server-side cancellation or permission to resend a previously unknown intent. No automatic resend occurs on reload, reconnect, mount or story playback.

## Managed routes and acceptance meaning

Register story/context-committed → authorized story view and story-focus intent → coordinated graph/source view through the integration registry. Coalesce polling views only, not accepted commands. Route scope includes review, snapshot, credential generation and connection lifetime; old responses cannot publish after logout/dispose/history/version change or clear a newer pending intent. One owned playback clock exists only while playing; teardown cancels it, aborts reads and removes listeners/routes. Mount/remount never publishes a story, launches work or sends another question. App shutdown retains normal durable records and existing process-owner behavior; the story layer owns no child process.

Acceptance asks whether a fresh reviewer can give a correct bounded before/after account, trace a real consequence, distinguish rationale/inference/unknown/execution and account for the rest of the PR, then investigate and resume without manual reconstruction or lost work. Controls, source coverage and citations alone cannot pass. Freeze the initial actual story/graph-first account before source inspection, compare/correct it afterward and retain misconceptions/effort. Native automatic delivery, meaningful attached-agent answer and unfamiliar-human observation remain separately assessed. Before accepting slice05/720p, reflect on actual code/structure, promote learned knowledge and close a fresh whole-current-product docs-only rebuild audit excluding plans/chat/code.


## Exact authoring core value contract

Status: accepted derivation for 05A; implementation and independent review evidence live in the slice checkpoint. This section defines DTO meaning, not executed acceptance. All objects are strict (unknown keys reject), IDs are nonempty strings of at most128 characters, hashes are lowercase SHA-256, repository paths are bounded relative paths, and revisions are safe nonnegative integers. Text preserves its exact validated bytes; object-key ordering alone is normalized. No author/timestamp/execution fields are accepted in authored inputs.

`StoryContent` contains `title`, optional `questionId`, optional paired `analysisId`/`corpusId`, ordered `captureIds`, `chapters`, `steps`, `claims`, `scope`, and `limitations`. `PublishStory` carries that value under `story`, plus command/review/snapshot IDs, `expectedStoryRevision`, `contextRevision`, and optional `replacesVersionId`. The replaces pointer, when supplied, must equal the current version; the server always records the actual previous version. Analysis/corpus must identify the same admitted result, including historical results. A question must belong to the same snapshot.

A chapter has `id`, `title`, `why`, `what`, `how` (each a claim ID), `example` (`scenarioKind`, `input`, `before`, `after`, with the last three claim IDs), and ordered nonempty `stepIds`. Every step occurs in exactly one chapter and has `id`, `headline`, nonempty `claimIds`, and `focus`. Every claim has `id`, `text`, `basis`, bounded `references`, optional `attribution` and `uncertainty`. Basis tags are `source_observation`, `static_relationship`, `supplied_rationale`, `inference`, `unknown`. Source observations require snapshot/semantic source references; static claims require a static node/edge reference plus source evidence matching each cited fact; rationale requires a supplied capture or attribution `author` (the server author) with no invented identity; inference requires evidence and uncertainty; unknown requires uncertainty and cannot claim source verification. Supplied rationale with captures remains attributed to their retained origin. Chapter/scope/step IDs resolve exactly; unattached claims reject.

Reference tags and fields are: `snapshot_source` (`anchor` in existing LF snapshot coordinates, `contentHash`); `semantic_source` (`corpusId`, complete `anchor` in owning UTF-16 coordinates); `static_node`/`static_edge` (`analysisId`, `factId`); `capture` (`captureId`, `contentHash`, optional paired UTF-16 `start`/`end`); `metadata` (`field` from title/prUrl/repository/prNumber/baseSha/headSha); `clarification` (`analysisId`, `clarificationId`, exact `revision`). No metadata body field exists. Hashes, nonempty ranges and retained membership are checked. A capture must be selected in the story's exact context revision and listed in its captureIds. Semantic/static/clarification references require the story's exact analysis basis; semantic corpus must match it.

Focus tags are `none`; `sources` with one/two snapshot-or-semantic source references (one per side) and exact primary `fileKey`; `structural` with primary `fileKey` and optional exact `hunkId`; `semantic` with one static reference and one/two exact source references. Source comparisons must match the primary changed-file paths for their sides; semantic focus must cite the selected node body/declaration or edge target/site, rather than an unrelated source. `none` is permitted only for a step whose claims are unknown. Scope tags are `explained` (`fileKey`, `chapterId`, `explanation`, nonempty evidence references) or `unexplained` (`fileKey`, `reason`). Exactly one row covers every graphFiles key, including copied comparisons and unsupported/empty files. Explained rows require source/static/capture/metadata support; the app validates identity, not prose truth. Limits are120 characters for titles/headlines,1200 for claim text/uncertainty,400 for scope explanation/reason,128 claims,32 steps,8 chapters,8 references per claim/scope/step and256 unique references overall. IDs, arrays and text are bounded before reference reads.

`PutStoryContext` carries command/review/snapshot IDs, `expectedContextRevision`, and `action`: strict `create` with `capture` (kind `Epic`/`Ticket`/`PR`, title, plain text, optional credential-free HTTP(S) originUrl, optional supersedesCaptureId), or `select` with ordered unique captureIds. Capture text is at most8KiB UTF-8; at most16 captures are selected. Supersession must name a currently selected capture. Content hash is SHA-256 of the exact capture text. The server creates captureId/createdAt/actorId; freshness is `captured` for a supplied URL and `unknown` otherwise. Create/select always advances context revision, including an unchanged explicit selection; no implicit no-op or deletion is inferred.

`storyQuerySchema`: reviewId/snapshotId, optional versionId/minimumStoryRevision/minimumContextRevision. `StoryView` returns those IDs, currentSnapshotId, currentAnalysisId, storyRevision/contextRevision, `currentVersion` summary, `version` (exact requested or latest, or null at revision zero), ordered version summaries, `newerVersion`, `newerContext`, `historicalAnalysis`, `staticNeighborhoodAvailable`, `basisUnavailable`, and `brokenEvidence` (bounded references/reasons). A missing requested version yields `story_version_unavailable`; unmet minima yield `revision_unavailable`. Version records include reviewId/snapshotId, server versionId/revision/hash/author/time/previousVersionId, contextRevision and exact StoryContent. Author includes actorId/kind and attachmentId/generation for agents. Initial `seriesAuthor` is immutable, including across user corrections.

`storyContextQuerySchema`: reviewId/snapshotId, optional contextRevision/captureId. `StoryContextView` returns currentContextRevision, selectedRevision, selectedCaptureIds, captures and missingKinds. Default returns only selected captures; exact captureId can retrieve a removed historical capture, clearly separate from selected IDs. An unavailable revision/capture is `story_context_unavailable`. Revision zero is the immutable empty selection. Captures and versions have no mutable selection side effect.

`StoryReceipt` contains commandId/actorId/state/reviewId/snapshotId/storyRevision/contextRevision, bounded result (new versionId/captureId when applicable), and optional typed error. Errors are `invalid_request`, `unauthorized`, `stale_attachment`, `source_unavailable`, `command_conflict`, `story_reference_invalid`, `story_structure_invalid`, `story_scope_invalid`, `story_context_conflict`, `story_conflict`, `story_author_conflict`, `story_storage_limit`, `story_version_unavailable`, `story_context_unavailable`, `revision_unavailable`. Malformed envelopes throw invalid_request before a receipt; valid rejected domain commands persist a receipt. Readback of unavailable/corrupt referenced data reports brokenEvidence without substitution; new writes reject.

### Retained evidence facade and atomic owner

The public `store.storyEvidence(token, query)` accepts review/snapshot IDs, optional paired analysis/corpus IDs and up to256 unique strict references. It authenticates and authorizes using the existing owner's credential/attachment checks, reads immutable snapshot and admitted analysis through public read/source ports, then returns a normalized `StoryEvidence` account of valid references plus invalid references/reasons, selected analysis marker, `basisUnavailable`, and `facts` (exact static reference with retained semantic anchors and separately normalized `snapshotAnchors` for focus validation). It never accepts caller-supplied facts, a path to read, an unadmitted preparation result or new source bytes. The story store receives this owner-created resolver as a private port; callers cannot supply an evidence bundle to command publication. Indexed maps cover source/file/fact membership; semantic source text is fetched once per unique identity. Public `validateSemanticAnchor(source, anchor)` accepts retained source content/hash/snapshot/side/path and an anchor, returns boolean only, and rejects identity/hash/empty/out-of-range or mismatched line coordinates. The shared pure semantic coordinate validator preserves CRLF, U2028/U2029 and UTF-16 offset semantics and is reused by graph clarifications. For every cited static fact, the owner resolves its semantic anchors against the exact retained corpus source, validates parser coordinates, and retains those anchors unchanged. When that same snapshot/side/path/hash and identical text also exists in the snapshot source manifest, the owner separately computes its LF-based start/end line span from the semantic UTF-16 offsets (end exclusive). These normalized snapshot-source reference values appear in `facts.snapshotAnchors`; absent snapshot text yields no snapshot mapping, never a guessed anchor. LF indexes use only U+000A; parser U+2028/U+2029/CR-only boundaries do not create snapshot LF lines, and CRLF/astral offsets are preserved. Domain static-claim/focus matching compares semantic references only with the semantic anchors and snapshot references only with these owner-normalized snapshot anchors, never directly with parser line numbers. A same-body/hash match is required before normalization. Context capture resolution stays with the story owner and exact context selection. Public `store.analysis.marker(token, {reviewId, snapshotId})` returns only reviewId/snapshotId/currentSnapshotId/analysisGeneration/graphRevision/selectedAnalysisId after existing credential, review and retained-snapshot authorization. It never loads corpus/inventory/analysis/preparation bodies. Story evidence uses this marker for current-selection labels even when unrelated retained analysis bodies are missing. Authored-basis resolution uses a separate strict public `store.analysis.evidence(token, {reviewId, snapshotId, analysisId})` read (`analysisEvidenceQuerySchema`, AnalysisEvidenceQuery/AnalysisEvidence). After the same credential/review/snapshot authorization, it requires an admitted job for that exact analysis ID and returns only reviewId/snapshotId/analysisId, that job’s retained corpus account and analysis result, and clarifications belonging to that analysis. It scans retained job records to locate the exact admitted job. It does not load unrelated semantic/preparation bodies, the current preparation projection, a newer corpus or unrelated inventory. Missing authored records remain analysis_record_unavailable. The general AnalysisView/query projection keeps its existing current-preparation semantics; it is not the story-evidence dependency. Historical publication/readback therefore remains available when only a newer corpus is missing, while absence of the story’s own corpus remains explicit broken evidence. Exact authored-basis reads classify only known retained-data failures (`analysis_record_unavailable` or `source_unavailable`) as basisUnavailable; invalid identity checks yield story_reference_invalid. Referenced bodies remain immutable in story readback with explicit brokenEvidence/basisUnavailable. The retained-analysis record owner classifies malformed stored JSON as analysis_record_unavailable at its parse boundary; unrelated SQL or programming failures are not reclassified. Authentication/stale-attachment and unexpected storage/programming errors propagate, never become an invented empty fact set.

`store.story.command(token, command)`, `.query(token, query)` and `.context(token, query)` are the only story write/read facade. Bounded validation/canonical encoding and immutable evidence reads happen before `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`; credential/review/snapshot membership, current attachment, series author, command identity, CAS and body quota are checked inside the short transaction. Authorization and agent series ownership precede receipt replay, including exact retry. Receipts are globally collision-checked with existing receipt namespaces in both directions: every legacy command ingress rejects a command ID already owned by a story receipt, and story ingress rejects IDs in legacy receipts. Existing receipt meanings are unchanged. Caller IDs beginning `story-internal:`, `analysis-admit:`, `import-admit:` or `attach:` are reserved even though this core creates no internal authoring command. Canonical JSON recursively sorts object keys, omits absent optional properties through strict schema parsing, preserves array order and exact text, and hashes the validated story payload. Receipt fingerprints hash canonical command plus full credential-derived principal; changed principal/payload conflicts.

One SQLite connection owned by createStore creates `story_heads` (review/snapshot primary key, independent counters/current version/series author), `story_captures` (immutable captureId and body/bytes), `story_contexts` (review/snapshot/revision selection/body/bytes), `story_versions` (versionId, review/snapshot/revision/body/bytes), and `story_receipts` (globally checked command ID, actor/principal fingerprint and immutable receipt). Unique revision indexes enforce immutable history. No table read crosses into analysis storage; no second connection, execution lease or wake is acquired. New body quota sums canonical capture/context/version bytes once per immutable body across the review, at most8MiB (owner tests may lower only); receipts/heads are excluded. Exact quota passes and one byte more rejects atomically. The initial empty context is implicit and consumes no body quota. Rejection stores only its receipt; historical bodies and current pointers remain unchanged.

Gateway adds POST `/api/story/commands`, GET `/api/story` and GET `/api/story/context` using those schemas. MCP adds `story_get`, `story_context_get`, `story_publish` on the existing attached capability, deriving reviewId from the launch for publication. Context remains owner-only gateway ingress. Story publication does not emit the addressed-question route, wake a worker or fulfill a question.
