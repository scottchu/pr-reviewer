## The smallest useful story

A reviewer should be able to explain the actual change, its purpose or the limits of known purpose, one concrete before/after example, and an important consequence without assembling the narrative from scattered source. A short authored story leads with graph/source highlights and explains those highlights in plain English. It accounts for supporting files and exposes unknowns. It is not a generated call graph with decorative prose, a correctness verdict or an execution report.

The first implementation supports one selected story version per retained review snapshot, at most eight chapters and thirty-two steps. A useful initial story usually has three chapters: the reviewer-visible change, the important mechanism/consequence, and supporting changes/evidence/remaining questions. That is an authoring guideline, not a hard-coded generic story that fits every PR. Every chapter includes a brief why, what/how and concrete example. An unsupported why is explicitly unknown or attributed to its author; it is never inferred as fact from a function name or citation. A whole-PR account may admit that some files remain unexplained.

A step presents its matching plain-language account beside a compact graph/source focus, with both visible in one coherent application state. The reading order gives meaning before expanded source bodies; graph-led means that the account points to real highlighted evidence, not that code must precede its explanation. It may focus a verified static relation, a source/file/hunk comparison or a context-backed explanation. Authored rationale never becomes a compiler edge. Preserve before-left/after-right, red removal, green addition and yellow modification with non-color labels. An absent side stays absent; no before/after mode toggle or API/Helpers orientation selector is added. Repeated import-header excerpts are poor explanations: select actual relevant source/body spans and explain the decision they support.

## Explanation and investigation requirements learned from use

A useful static neighborhood can accurately show a new constructor dependency and unchanged neighbors while leaving the actual user consequence, request policy and lifecycle unexplained. Preserve that factual success and its limits. The story must supply an inspectable source-backed account of the decision and a concrete before/after user example, separating authored explanation from verified static bindings. An edge does not prove request authorization, teardown, business purpose or observed execution; missing support stays unknown. A source-corrected account is not evidence that the initial graph-first presentation already conveyed it.

Complete file inventory is necessary but cannot be the whole-change explanation. Use existing chapter membership and primary scope accounts to group the change by responsibility, with a concise source-backed reason for each group and visible unexplained material. Supporting docs/config/tests remain part of that account. No separate grouping taxonomy, inferred ownership engine or per-file semantic edge is needed. Acceptance requires a fresh reviewer to summarize central/supporting responsibilities and one real consequence without assembling a narrative from filenames and long function bodies.

Exploration initially shows issues associated with the exact selected declaration or source path; retain clear all-issues access and counts. Group only existing diagnostic codes/causes and distinguish incomplete-context findings from other reported issues without inventing severity or suppressing them. Entries with no reliable selected-source association remain explicitly unassigned/global, never falsely unrelated or resolved. This is a bounded projection over admitted facts and their existing coverage, not new analysis or a complete unresolved ledger claim beyond the returned data.

The source inspector foregrounds the exact selected span with side/path/commit/corpus/hash and applicable coordinate format, then offers full-file expansion. Preserve context needed to interpret the span and an explicit return to the original focus/scroll/selection. Highlighted-span selection and source availability are separate from question anchor permission; unchanged semantic-only context does not become a fabricated legacy snapshot anchor. The goal is less filtering and source scanning while keeping every available fact, warning and source route inspectable.

## Reading hierarchy and explicit investigation intent

**Design amendment:** 05D, derived from an actual graph-first review of candidate `67b24f3`. The central conceptual account was correct, but a fresh agent rated overall cognitive effort burdensome. Large excerpts appeared before their explanation; chapter details repeated current claims; whole-change scope exposed machine tuple keys; graph placement required checking the relationship list; inspected source and the independent question anchor were easy to confuse. This is qualitative single-agent evidence, not a human study, a false-edge finding or a measured time saving. The following is the corrective interaction contract; delivery remains gated separately.

The primary job is to understand a change and its consequence before deciding what to investigate. Preserve the incumbent React surfaces, colors, typography, evidence and subsystem owners. Reduce competing presentation, rather than adding another summary layer, generated rationale or a new information model.

- A selected step starts with its chapter/step identity, the current plain-language account and its consequence or example when authored. Show the claim's source-observation/static/inference/unknown attribution and material uncertainty beside that account. Do not fabricate a consequence when absent. Compact focus labels and an evidence disclosure follow the explanation; an unopened long source body must not push the entire explanation below the story panel's first viewport on desktop or narrow layouts. The reviewer may scroll the page to the story panel; this is not a promise that every global control fits above it.
- Render each exact claim ID once within the current account and expanded chapter-detail area. Chapter details add why/what/how/example material not already displayed, with clear labels; if already displayed, refer to its existing location or omit the duplicate rather than repeat it. Distinct claims with similar wording remain distinct authored claims; presentation must not rewrite their meaning or treat textual similarity as identity. Missing rationale and uncertainty stay visible. Compact claim provenance remains available without opening the full source body.
- Before/after evidence is available through a clearly labeled, initially collapsed disclosure showing side, path and selected range. Opening it presents the exact selected span, then an explicit full-source route, retaining existing provenance, LF versus UTF-16 coordinate labels and missing-side explanations. Evidence disclosure, source reading and chapter detail reading pause playback. An explicitly opened source remains readable until the reviewer changes focus or closes it; playback does not auto-expand a wall of code. No retained source, reference or scope entry is discarded to make the page shorter.
- The whole-change account starts with existing chapter responsibility groups and counts, their authored meaning, and an explicit unexplained group. Each expandable group lists every primary file exactly once with its human-readable repository path, change status where retained, and authored explanation. Distinguish renamed before/after paths when available. Resolve labels through the exact retained file identity; expose the original machine key in technical details, never use tuple serialization as the main file label. Unknown identity remains visibly unavailable rather than guessed. Group membership is the existing authored chapter assignment, not a new dependency/ownership classifier. Counts reconcile to the complete immutable scope. A generic responsibility is not proof of understanding that file's actual change; authoring must supply a specific source-backed role or declare the gap unexplained. The app does not invent those explanations.
- A static graph uses compact function identities, side/change/context labels and legible directed caller-to-callee relationships as its default. Function bodies are available on explicit inspection rather than occupying every node. The selected function, its direct incoming/outgoing relations and unknowns must be recognizable without deciphering source boxes or consulting another list to discover direction. Preserve before-left/after-right and all admitted relationship IDs, call-site evidence and limits. Placement is a layout, not execution order, lifecycle order, causality or a fabricated edge. Cycles/unknown calls remain truthful; this bounded refinement does not add a new graph algorithm or increase analysis coverage. The accessible relationship list remains a peer navigation route, not the only explanation of canvas direction.
- Evidence inspection and question composition have independent, persistently visible source labels: snapshot, side, path and range. If they differ, say so next to the investigation/composer handoff. An explicit **Use this range for question** intent is available only for an exact question-eligible retained snapshot anchor. It previews old and proposed anchor, preserves text and parent question, and delegates through the existing workspace/draft owner. For a nonempty unsent draft, require explicit confirmation of that anchor replacement; cancel leaves everything unchanged. This applies to the active exploration draft and never overwrites the captured original return-frame draft. An in-flight or unresolved pending command remains immutable and blocks retargeting, with an explanation. Confirmed submitted text does not become an unsent replacement draft. A semantic-only/unchanged source without an owner-validated snapshot mapping cannot be used as an invented question anchor; explain the unavailable action. Opening a reference, focusing a graph node, publication, background refresh or reading source never implicitly applies this intent.

The one-row draggable player, step number, keyboard/focus behavior, reading pause, mobile synchronized Flow/Diff, red removal/green addition/yellow modification with non-color labels, exact story/source/draft return frame, private recovery and authority contracts remain binding. New disclosure or anchor-confirmation controls need accessible names, keyboard operation and visible focus. In narrow layouts a long story title may wrap, while the adjacent Flow/Diff labels remain on one line and retain their controls. Keep independent UI critique separate from tests and screenshot geometry. A successful correction requires a fresh graph/story-first account frozen before source/key, source comparison, an explicit cognitive-burden verdict and the meaningful question/reply/return journey; a shorter screenshot or passing DOM assertion alone does not establish understanding.

Groups use existing chapter membership and the authored what-claim, plus a separate unexplained group. `questionEvidenceProjection` reports ready, blocked or unavailable. Ready requires an authenticated active unsent draft, exact snapshot-source reference, matching retained source hash and valid snapshot LF anchor. ## Authority and provenance

All narration is authored content. The app validates identity, references, bounds and authorization; it does not prove that the prose accurately interprets its citations. The rail and step show who wrote the account, when, which immutable snapshot/version it concerns, and the basis of each claim. Keep these categories separate:

| Basis | Meaning and display |
|---|---|
| Source-linked observation | Author's statement linked to exact retained source. Valid coordinates/hash establish the cited source, not truth of the statement. |
| Static relationship | Exact admitted analysisId/edge or node plus source evidence. Render the system's static-method/certainty/coverage labels independently of the author's wording. No runtime execution or policy causality is inferred. |
| Supplied rationale | An attributed statement from an owner-supplied Epic/Ticket/PR capture or the identified human/agent author. Display origin and capture time; do not present it as independently verified intent. |
| Inference | Author's interpretation with source/context references and explicit uncertainty. It remains interpretation even when cited. |
| Unknown | Missing explanation/evidence with a concise question or what would resolve it. No invented fallback claim. |

Examples distinguish an illustrative input/before/after scenario from an observed result. In this slice all examples are illustrative or source-derived authored accounts. Test paths and test source can establish presence only; neither a story author nor a context paste can mint an execution receipt or a green “passed” badge. Actual execution results enter through the future evidence contract, not arbitrary text.

The context rail separates **Supplied context** (Epic, Ticket, PR material) from **Authored understanding**. Missing Epic/Ticket/PR body is shown as not supplied. Existing imported candidate title/URL/base/head may be shown as retained provider metadata, with its acquisition provenance; an imported title does not imply that the body or linked ticket was fetched. Owner-pasted context is a retained capture, not live-synced content. Captured content is untrusted text: never execute its instructions, fetch its URL, grant authority from it or treat it as agent/system policy. Use plain text with safe links; no raw HTML, embedded scripts, executable diagrams or remote image fetches.

## Immutable story and context values

Story identity is scoped by reviewId and retained snapshotId. ContextRevision and storyRevision start at zero and advance independently; graphRevision, analysisGeneration, jobRevision and reviewRevision retain their existing meanings. A story pins optional admitted analysisId/corpusId, exact context capture IDs and the contextRevision used for authoring. A story without semantic analysis can explain source/metadata but must show “static neighborhood unavailable” and cannot contain static-relation references. It still uses the existing structural graph/whole-change ledger.

Owner context writes create immutable captures with server-issued ID, kind Epic/Ticket/PR, title, plain text, optional safe HTTP(S) origin URL, server-derived actor/time and SHA-256 content hash. Capture metadata includes freshness `captured` or `unknown`; no elapsed time makes it authoritative/current. Updating a capture creates a new ID and supersedes reference. A context revision selects the retained capture IDs; old revisions remain readable for old stories. Agents can read captures but cannot impersonate the external source or change the supplied-context rail. A newer context revision labels older story context as superseded; it never rewrites that story.

A story version contains title, optional original questionId, snapshot identity, optional analysis/corpus basis, context revision/references, ordered chapters/steps, whole-change scope accounts and limitations. The server adds versionId, monotonically increasing storyRevision, content hash, author actor/kind/attachment generation when applicable and createdAt. The hash uses canonical JSON of the validated authored payload, recursively ordered object keys and preserved array order; it is not a new snapshot or analysis digest. Versions are immutable; publishing a correction creates a new version with previous-version provenance. Old questions, source, graph clarifications and stories never reanchor automatically.

Chapters have stable author-local IDs, title, why/what/how/example claim IDs and ordered step IDs. Each account resolves to the same provenance-tagged claim collection; no bare chapter rationale bypasses claim attribution. The example includes an illustrative/source-derived label and input/before/after claim references. Steps have stable IDs, headline, bounded claim IDs and a focus descriptor. Claims contain plain text, one basis from the table, references and optional attribution/uncertainty. A focus names exact source anchor(s), structural file/hunk IDs or admitted semantic node/edge IDs; it contains no arbitrary node coordinates/edges/source bytes. One step has at most two primary source anchors, one per side, with a separately identified comparison. Text-only unknown steps explicitly have no source focus. A graph layer may highlight a maximum of twelve visible nodes using existing readable-neighborhood policy; other facts stay accessible.

Reference types are strict tagged values: retained snapshot source (snapshot/side/path/contentHash and exact line span); semantic source (snapshot/corpus plus the complete semantic UTF-16/line anchor); static node/edge (analysisId plus existing fact ID); supplied capture (captureId/contentHash and optional exact text range); retained PR metadata field; or clarification ID plus its exact revision and analysisId. No mutable current pointer, local file path, prose URL or guessed identifier substitutes for evidence. Snapshot LF-based line spans and semantic parser coordinates remain distinct formats; use their owning validators, not arithmetic conversion that breaks CRLF/U2028/astral text. Validate every static and semantic reference through retained authorized04 read ports. Clarification text remains attributed interpretation when cited.

Primary comparison/file keys and structural hunk IDs use the [exact structural identity recipes and complete vectors](persistence-compatibility.md#structural-comparison-identity-compatibility), including manual fallback and copied comparisons. Each primary changed-file ledger key from `graphFiles(snapshot)` has exactly one scope account: a primary chapter with a short explanation, or an explicit unexplained reason. A file may be referenced by other steps without increasing coverage. Preserve copy/rename comparison keys and repeated identical provider source occurrences; never deduplicate the stored manifest or confuse a copied comparison with its modified original. Existing hunk/disposition coverage is rendered from source, not recreated or claimed narrated by story counts. Missing/binary/empty/unsupported files remain accessible scope rows; no fabricated line1. More than512 primary files is explicitly unsupported by this bounded story contract, never silently truncated into a whole-PR claim.

## Authoring and read protocol

These implemented operations use strict public domain schemas, the existing SQLite owner, authenticated gateway ingress and the existing attached participant's MCP transport. No app-hosted model, API key, real-time service, new conversation or session fork is introduced. Context/story publication is a bounded durable value write, not a repository process job and does not acquire the shared execution lease.

| Operation | Input and apply semantics |
|---|---|
| PutStoryContext | User only: commandId, reviewId, snapshotId, expectedContextRevision and bounded capture create/supersede/selection intent. Validate retained snapshot and origin/text limits; append captures and context revision atomically. Agents/bootstrap cannot write supplied context. |
| PublishStory | User or active attached agent: commandId, reviewId, snapshotId, expectedStoryRevision, contextRevision, bounded complete story payload and optional replacesVersionId. Pin only retained source and admitted analysis. CAS storyRevision and the authoring context revision; reject stale context with `story_context_conflict`, stale story with `story_conflict`. Do not use unrelated graph/review/job revisions as CAS. |
| Story query | Authorized reviewId/snapshotId, optional exact versionId, minimumStoryRevision and minimumContextRevision. Return selected published version summary, requested exact version, current snapshot/analysis markers and explicit newer-version/context indicators; unmet minima are `revision_unavailable`. Query never moves a selected source/history pointer. |
| Story context query | Authorized reviewId/snapshotId and optional exact contextRevision/capture ID. Return bounded retained captures with provenance. No external fetch or arbitrary path read. |

Command IDs and author-local chapter/step/claim IDs are nonempty strings of at most128 characters; revisions are nonnegative integers. PutStoryContext has exactly one action: `create` with one capture (kind/title/text/optional originUrl/optional supersedesCaptureId), or `select` with an ordered list of retained capture IDs. Create atomically selects the new capture, replacing only its named superseded capture when present and preserving other selections; more than16 selected captures rejects. Select may remove a capture from the current rail but never deletes retained data. Duplicate/foreign/supersedes IDs reject. PublishStory supplies the complete authored value, not an arbitrary patch; every chapter/step/claim/focus/scope reference must resolve in that value or retained evidence. Example scenarioKind is `illustrative` or `source_derived`; neither author-controlled value permits observed-execution badges.

Each command has a bounded ID, canonical actor/payload fingerprint and durable receipt. Recheck authorization before receipt replay. Exact authorized retry returns the original result; changed actor/payload conflicts. Receipts contain commandId/actorId/applied-or-rejected, reviewId/snapshotId, storyRevision/contextRevision, optional new version/capture IDs, result and typed error. They are not graph/job receipts with a renamed revision. Append body, pointers and receipt in one short transaction after bounded validation/encoding; recheck retained membership/authorization/CAS inside. No transaction waits for an agent, browser, source process or external context.

Attached agents may publish only for their authorized review and current attachment generation, as derived from credentials. They may create an initial story or revise the story series they authored; its initial author remains the series owner when a user appends a correction. A user may supersede any version with preserved provenance. A different attached author receives `story_author_conflict`; the user can publish an attributed account through owner ingress instead. Transfer of series ownership and collaborative authorship are deferred, not silently inferred from attachment replacement. Bootstrap is never an author. Do not allow caller-supplied actor/timestamp or `verified:true`. Replacing attachment does not alter story authorship, and revoked generation cannot read/write/replay through the former capability.

A new graph clarification or analysis request does not conflict with a story write. A story may cite a retained earlier admitted analysis explicitly; publication labels that basis historical if it is no longer selected. Source history advancing likewise does not invalidate a historical story. Publication updates the snapshot's available current story pointer but never auto-selects it in an already-reading client. The client announces a newer story/context and offers an explicit open action. If an older reference is unavailable/corrupt, reject new publication; existing readback reports broken evidence rather than substituting current source or invented text.

The existing8 MiB gateway envelope is not permission to accept unbounded authored output.

Exact boundary passes; the next new write rejects atomically with `story_storage_limit`, retaining prior history. Owner test limits may only lower. No silent eviction, pruning of cited evidence or automatic compaction of a story into different content. Receipts, private browser state and the rest of SQLite are separate from this body quota; this is not an all-database resource guarantee.

Source/semantic evidence validation uses bounded public ports and indexed lookups once per unique reference. Retained reference facts are immutable and may be checked outside the transaction; authority/CAS/body equality recheck inside. Domain owns content policy and transitions, application owns view/player state, UI renders. Existing import/analysis process leases, schemas and resource limits stay unchanged. Restart reads authoritative versions/receipts without rerunning acquisition, analysis or agent turns.

## Player, reading and exploration

The slim bottom-center player has one row: drag handle, previous, replay/pause, next, step number and chapter selector. Icons have accessible names/tooltips; text carries story information. Keep44px coarse-pointer targets even when visual chrome is slim. Controls remain operable at narrow width; chapter labels may shorten with full accessible names, never hide step/navigation. A keyboard move/reset alternative makes dragging optional. Position is private, clamped inside the visible viewport/safe-area, restored on resize and resettable; dragging never navigates or alters shared state.

Player state is application-owned: exact review/snapshot/storyVersion, chapterId/stepId/index, mode (`paused`, `playing`, `exploring`, `ended`), elapsed step time, highlight/displayed evidence, selected comparison/source and an optional exploration return frame. Initial load is paused. Explicit Replay replays the current step's highlight from time zero and continues in story order across chapter boundaries; while playing the same control is Pause. Previous/next moves one step in global story order, chapter choice selects that chapter's first step, and either pauses for reading. Step number shows the global position/total with the current chapter label. Default playback advances after eight seconds of visible active time per step; the final step ends paused/ended, with no loop. No duration controls, audio or authored executable timing language are added.

Reading actions pause: focusing/selecting narration (not focus inside the playback controls), user scrolling the source/context rail, activating evidence, dragging the player, switching Flow/Diff, hiding the page or losing the active review connection. Preserve elapsed time; no hidden-tab catch-up. Reduced-motion uses immediate highlights and starts paused, and never removes information. Replay is an explicit time-zero action; “Resume story” after exploration restores its saved cursor/elapsed value in paused mode, so it cannot advance before the reviewer is ready.

Entering exploration first freezes one return frame containing the exact story version/chapter/step/elapsed cursor, graph focus/expanded IDs/viewport, displayed source/comparison/selection and original draft identity/anchor/text. Exploring other nodes, source, chapters through ordinary source navigation or supporting files does not overwrite that frame; explicit story navigation exits exploration after restoring/confirming pending draft rules. Resume restores the frame, not the latest current graph or newest story. Exploration can have its own private selection/draft; never replace a dirty or uncertain submitted draft with the saved one. Preserve both scoped drafts and require explicit choice/reconciliation when switching which composer is active. The pending-submission recovery contract below requires exact unchanged retry/readback before retargeting, including after reload.

Story focus and question draft anchor are distinct. Playback may change displayed evidence through an explicit user play/step action, but cannot silently retarget an existing question/draft. “Ask about this step” is a separate intent and respects pending/dirty composer state. Import completion, analysis admission, context/story publication and polling never move the story cursor, active source snapshot, private selection or draft. Explicit user history/version change pauses and stores the old private frame; opening a newer story starts paused rather than borrowing old chapter IDs.

Basic mobile Flow (graph/story) and Diff (source) presentations share the same application state. Switching is reading/exploration, not a second story instance; preserve cursor, pause, return frame and draft. Before/after identities stay available on narrow screens without a toggle that changes source identity. Full mobile tuning, semantic zoom, sophisticated motion and4K polish remain later scope.

Retain a bounded tab-session view checkpoint (sessionStorage, at most64 KiB encoded UTF-8) of private IDs/cursor/position/return-frame and unsent draft text (at most8 KiB per active/return draft), never bearer credentials, full source, story/capture bodies or process data. This is tab-local recovery, not account sync or shared canonical state. On reload validate against authorized retained records and pause before showing; unavailable/stale selection gets explicit recovery without guessing an anchor. Oversize unsent drafts remain complete in memory, are omitted from this view checkpoint and visibly marked unavailable after reload; never truncate a draft. If storage is unavailable/full, unsent view state can remain in memory with an explicit recovery-unavailable message. This best-effort rule does not permit sending an uncheckpointed question.

### Pending question recovery across reload

The review application owns the public recovery seam, separate from story publication and the import bridge. The minimum contract is `getQuestionRecovery()` (safe state/identity), `recoverPendingQuestion()` (explicit action under the current authenticated credential) and the existing `ask` operation extended with write-ahead persistence. These are current application public exports; the earlier028 memory-only submission implementation did not provide them. UI passes intent through them; it never reads/writes pending storage directly or reconstructs an AskQuestion command from a restored draft. No new server job, inbox category, credential service or receipt endpoint is required: retry uses the existing `/api/commands` receipt path; readback uses the authorized retained-review query.

At most one unresolved question command is retained per tab. ### Browser scope compatibility vector

Schema1 scope uses the browser UI/proxy-facing `location.origin`, not the separate internal gateway listener URL. An injected test origin is used literally.
